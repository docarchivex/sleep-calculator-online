03 Calculation Accuracy Limitations

Our sleep calculator uses a generalized 90-minute sleep cycle model based on published averages from peer-reviewed sleep science research. While this model is widely recognized in sleep medicine, it is important to understand the following inherent limitations:

  • Individual Variation: Sleep cycle length varies from person to person and can range from 70 to 120 minutes — the 90-minute figure is a population median.
  • Night-to-Night Variation: Even in the same individual, sleep cycle length varies between nights based on sleep pressure, circadian phase, and other factors.
  • Sleep Latency Approximation: The sleep latency value (time to fall asleep) used in our calculations is self-reported and approximate. Actual sleep onset time varies.
  • REM Estimates: Our REM and deep sleep duration estimates are based on typical population distributions and do not reflect your actual sleep architecture.
  • Sleep Quality Score: This score is a relative, directional indicator only. It does not reflect actual EEG-measured sleep quality or clinical sleep parameters.
  • Age Group Generalization: Sleep recommendations by age group are population-level guidelines. Individual needs within each age group vary significantly.

04 Factors Not Accounted For

Our sleep calculator does not account for the following factors that significantly influence individual sleep quality and optimal timing:

  • Diagnosed sleep disorders (insomnia, sleep apnea, narcolepsy, REM sleep behavior disorder, restless legs syndrome)
  • Prescription medications and their effects on sleep architecture and timing
  • Mental health conditions including anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, and PTSD
  • Chronic pain conditions that disrupt sleep continuity
  • Hormonal changes including pregnancy, menopause, and thyroid conditions
  • Alcohol and substance use effects on sleep cycles
  • Shift work disorder and chronic circadian rhythm disruption
  • Jet lag and acute circadian phase disruption
  • Genetic variations in sleep need (e.g., DEC2 short sleeper gene)
  • Environmental factors including light, noise, temperature, and altitude

If any of these factors apply to you, our general recommendations may be significantly less accurate than for healthy adults with regular sleep patterns. Please consult a healthcare professional for personalized guidance.
